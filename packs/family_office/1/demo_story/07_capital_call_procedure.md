# 07 - Capital-call and distribution procedure

Owner of the document: Sara Lim, Operations Manager. Version 3, last reviewed 14 months ago. Amounts are illustrative.

## Part 1. The procedure as written

### Purpose
Make sure every capital call is noticed, verified, funded and paid on or before its due date, and that every distribution is recorded and its effect on unfunded commitments is correct.

### Capital calls
1. **Receive.** Managers send notices to the shared mailbox capital-calls@example.test. The agreements require ten business days' notice.
2. **Log within one working day.** The Operations Manager enters the call in Capital_Call_Tracker.xlsx: fund, amount, currency, due date, notice date.
3. **Verify within two working days.** Check the call against the fund agreement (is it within the commitment? is the fee element permitted? do the payment instructions match those on file?). Never change payment details on the strength of an email; confirm any change by calling a known contact.
4. **Tell Treasury and Finance.** The Operations Manager informs the Treasurer and the Finance Lead the same day it is verified.
5. **Fund.** The Treasurer arranges cash: money-market redemption (one working day) and, for USD calls, FX purchase (two working days).
6. **Release.** The Finance Lead releases the payment in Northgate Custody Online; the Treasurer co-signs. Calls above USD 1 million also need the Head of Investments' approval. Release is at least one working day before the due date and always before the 15:00 cut-off.
7. **Confirm.** The Operations Manager marks the row Paid when the debit appears in the bank file, and keeps the confirmation.
8. **Corrected notices.** A corrected notice replaces the earlier one. Mark the earlier row Superseded and link it to the new row. Pay only the current one.
9. **Report.** Notified calls appear in the pack's funding page as known obligations.

### Distributions
1. Log the notice in the Distributions tab: amount, currency, split between recallable and non-recallable.
2. Match the credit to the bank file.
3. Add recallable amounts back to the unfunded commitment. Non-recallable amounts do not change unfunded.
4. Estimated distributions are never used in the base case for funding.

## Part 2. What actually happens

| Written | In practice | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Ten business days' notice | Median five working days left when the email arrives (last 12 months, 34 notices, owner estimate). Some notices are dated earlier than the email that carries them. | Little time to fund, especially in USD. |
| Log within one working day | Median two working days. Eleven of the 34 were logged three or more working days after receipt, usually when Sara was away or busy with the pack. | Treasury hears late. |
| Single mailbox | Some managers write to a named person who left or to Sara directly. The September Harborview IV notice first went to a previous contact address. | Notices are missed or arrive twice. |
| Tell Treasury same day | Often a chat message or a hallway remark. No written hand-over; Tom sometimes finds out from the bank balance. | Funding is planned late. |
| Fund and release one working day early | Frequently released on the due date itself, before the 15:00 cut-off. | Risk of late payment and manager interest. |
| Mark superseded by link | Done by typing, sometimes not done. | Risk of double counting known obligations. |
| Distribution recallable split | The split is read from the notice if it is clear; otherwise left blank. | Unfunded may be wrong. |
| Procedure reviewed yearly | Last reviewed 14 months ago. | The written text no longer matches practice. |

## Example: the September Harborview IV call

- Notice dated Tuesday 1 September 2026. The first copy went to a previous contact address. A resend reached the mailbox on Tuesday 8 September, 16:42.
- Due Tuesday 15 September. That left five working days.
- Sara logged it on Thursday 10 September, two working days after receipt, and told Tom that day.
- Tom bought USD on Friday 11 September (settles T+2, on the due date) and Finance released the payment on the morning of the 15th.
- It was paid on time, but with no margin. See 10_call_notice_example.eml and 11_meeting_notes_cash_planning.txt.

## What the gap tells module 1 and module 2

- Module 1: the problem is not that Sara is slow. The notices arrive late and the hand-offs are informal.
- Module 2: the call data has no fixed arrival time, an unstructured source, one process owner, and no system link to the bank. These become gaps for module 3.
