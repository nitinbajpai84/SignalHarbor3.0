ILLUSTRATIVE: invented for demonstration

# 15 - Data governance issues, in the office's own words

Illustrative Lakeside Family Office (ILFO). Quotes are invented and attributed to the cast. These are the notes people put on the board in module 1 and the answers they give in module 2. Nobody is blamed: the issues are about definitions, owners and access.

## 1. Three versions of "return"

- Arjun: "In my workbook return is the change in value since last quarter. For Marlowe I use time-weighted. For the private funds I use the net IRR from the manager's statement. All three go in a column called Return."
- Mei: "When I say return to the committee I mean net of fees, time-weighted. The old pack page showed value change. I did not know until I asked."
- Lina: "Finance sends me a number called return. I do not know which one."

Gap: no approved definition, no owner. Related term: K19 Data definitions with a named owner.

## 2. Nobody owns the benchmark choices

- Mei: "I picked the MSCI World index for Marlowe years ago. I think."
- Arjun: "The manager's report uses a different global index, and I switched once when the numbers looked odd. Nothing stopped me, and nothing recorded it."
- Sara: "There is a benchmark column in the fee list. It is part of the fee list nobody owns."

Gap: choices live in three places (Performance_Workbook.xlsx, Mei's slide, manager reports). Five of eight holdings have none. No rule on who approves or changes one. Related terms: K15, K16, K17.

## 3. Personal data in the pay spreadsheet

- Lina: "The pay workbook has names, salary bands and bonus amounts. It sits in the HR folder, but the folder is open to Finance because that is how Arjun's file reaches me."
- Arjun: "I paste the performance figures into the same workbook so Lina can see them. I can see the pay columns while I do that."

Gap: personal data is not separated from performance data. Owner of access: none named. Bank narratives (S01) also name family members: personal class "not sure".

## 4. Who may see what

- Mei: "I can open everything. I am not sure I should."
- Sara: "Manager statements are in my folder. Anyone with the shared drive link can open them."
- Wei: no answer recorded.

Gap: no access list for the performance workbook, pay workbook or manager statements. Anyone with the drive link can open some of them.

## 5. The data comes from many places and nobody has signed any of them off

- Index levels: pasted from a market data vendor's download (Bloomberg Data License, illustrative) and from an index provider's file. No licence note is on file for redistribution inside the pack.
- Manager statements: PDF attachments by email, 30 to 60 days after quarter-end.
- Custodian daily file and the administrator's NAV portal: downloaded by Arjun.
- Excel: the performance workbook, the pay workbook and the capital-call tracker.

Gap: no list of authorised sources, no named owner for the market-data feed, no agreed arrival time.

## 6. The fee list again

Three copies, no owner. Unchanged from the first workshop; it now folds into governance as "key list with no owner".

## What the board does with this

In module 1 the issues form one cluster: data governance. In module 2 they become K19 and the unknowns in the catalogue (owner, personal data, access, authorised source). The owners are asked: who owns this definition, who may see it, which source is authoritative.
